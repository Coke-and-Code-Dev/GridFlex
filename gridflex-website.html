The problem

The Dutch grid is full. Fleets still have to go electric.

Businesses in congested areas often cannot get a bigger connection for years. At the same time, zero-emission zones are pushing delivery fleets to electric now.

The question a facility manager actually has: we can’t get a bigger connection before the 2030s. Can we add chargers under the one we have, with what schedule, and how confident can we be?

The idea

One connection. One limit. Two loads.

The building and the fleet share one connection limit. When vans plug in at the end of the day, their demand stacks on top of the building’s own use.

Ten vans on 11 kW chargers draw 110 kW. On a 100 kW connection that is already over the limit, before the building uses any power.

Local congestion can delay an upgrade for years. So the question becomes: how much can you electrify by changing when you charge?

How it works · Methodology

Locate → Plan → Stress-test → Explain & decide

01

Locate

Type a Dutch postcode and see the area’s grid situation from the operators’ own capacity map.

  • Congestion colour for new demand
  • Queued requests and MW waiting
  • Expected relief year and listed grid projects
02

Plan

Describe the site and fleet. An optimiser builds one charging timetable for every van that stays under the limit on a cautious day.

  • Building load from your 15-minute meter data, or a bill-based estimate
  • Arrivals, departures, kWh per van, charger power
  • Optional battery and off-peak capacity
03

Stress-test

The fixed timetable is replayed on held-out days it was never planned on.

  • How often the site goes over the limit
  • When, and by how much
  • Which vans would leave short of charge
04

Explain & decide

An explanation agent turns the computed results into plain language. The operator approves or rejects.

  • Verdict · what changed · what to check
  • Ask follow-up questions or try a what-if
  • Export the schedule as CSV

Plan on some customers, test on others

Realistic days are split 70/30 by customer, never by day. The plan is built on a cautious 90th-percentile profile of one group and judged only on the other, so the risk describes days the planner has never seen.

Your data, or a business like yours

With 15-minute meter readings, GridFlex plans and tests on the site’s own history. Without them, it scales synthetic sector profiles to the monthly kWh on your energy bill.

From one depot to a portfolio

Upload a list of sites and get a portfolio report: which depots are ready, which carry risk, and which need a schedule change or a battery before you invest.

Honesty and limitations

What GridFlex is, and isn’t

  • Building use is synthetic unless you upload meter data, and fleet requests are assumptions you can edit.
  • The capacity map is indicative and per area, not per connection. Always check with your grid operator.
  • Extra off-peak capacity is a what-if. Real contract terms come from the grid operator.
  • A feasible plan is not a grid-operator approval, contract compliance or a guarantee.
  • One fixed timetable is replayed on every test day. A real controller could react on the day, so the risk numbers are on the cautious side.
  • Rounding: integer kW per 15-minute slot; energy needs rounded up to 0.25 kWh.
  • Synthetic profiles condition on month and consumption level, not on specific weather.
  • GridFlex only advises. It never switches chargers or equipment.
